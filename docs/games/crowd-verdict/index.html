Crowd Verdict: Refund or Reject?

Preparing the jury room…

Inside the jury room: rules & assumptions +

A crowd decision is not necessarily the right decision.

You are one juror on a fictional second-hand marketplace. Start with 10 tokens. Pay 1 token to unlock a case, inspect the evidence, then choose Refund or No Refund. Your vote and 2, 4, 6 or 8 simulated votes form an odd-sized jury, so there is always a majority.

Agree with the majority and receive 2 tokens: a net gain of 1 after the entry fee. Disagree and receive no reward: a net loss of 1. There is no additional penalty. Stop whenever you like; a paid entry fee is not returned. If you finish during the vote animation, your submitted ballot is settled before the report appears. Spending your last token still lets you finish that paid case.

How the other jurors vote

For each case, every simulated juror independently draws a type: 65% normal, 20% buyer-leaning, 15% seller-leaning. Buyer-leaning jurors always vote Refund; seller-leaning jurors always vote No Refund. Normal jurors respond probabilistically to the evidence:

Default probability that a normal juror votes Refund
ConditionRefund probability
Good condition5%
Lightly damaged30%
Damaged70%
Badly damaged95%

The jury’s actual types and ballots stay hidden until after your vote. All cases, types and ballots are generated from the replay code before play. Animations only reveal those saved votes; they never change them to make you win or lose. Replay the same cases to try a different strategy. A different vote can change the majority because your ballot counts.

A full ten-case session includes all ten product categories, all four condition levels and all four panel sizes, in a seeded random order. Remaining condition and panel-size slots are also randomly drawn. This ensures variety in a short game; juror types and votes are still independently drawn, with no target winning rate.

The illustrative evidence benchmark

Every listing promises good condition. For this game, undisclosed substantial damage (Damaged or Badly damaged) supports a refund; Good condition and Lightly damaged support No Refund. The classification stays hidden while you decide. This simplified benchmark is a teaching rule, not a real consumer-protection standard or an assessment of legal refund entitlement.

Use Tab and Enter to operate every control. Pause or skip the ballot animation at any time. Leaving the tab pauses it; reduced-motion preferences remove moving effects and show ballots immediately. No decision has a time limit.

This is a standalone fictional crowd-judging simulation. It does not reproduce the theoretical model in Rushed Justice. Tokens are fictional game points, have no monetary value and cannot be redeemed. No account, real payment or backend is involved.

The website’s previously enabled Cloudflare Web Analytics measures visits and performance. Gameplay does not depend on analytics or external APIs.
