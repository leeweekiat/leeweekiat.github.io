Solo or Team?

Your marketplace is getting ready.

Behind the game: rules & assumptions +

A teaching abstraction, with visible decisions.

Inspired by When Should Gig Platforms Allow Teams? by Wee Kiat Lee and Dan Luo. This is an independently specified teaching simulation. Its numbers, opportunities and outcomes are illustrative; they are not estimates or claims about the paper’s results.

  1. Commit first. Choose a policy before any individual opportunity or private worker cost is revealed. You know only the broad marketplace conditions.
  2. Workers respond. A suitable lead tries the job. If the lead cannot take it, an eligible secondary worker can create a transaction through reassignment. If the lead tries and fails, a secondary worker can rescue that existing transaction through backup.
  3. Permission has a trade-off. Under Case-by-Case Approval, the lead compares the expected benefit of proposing with its private cost and the available alternative. Greater requirements raise client consent probability, proposal burden and convex processing cost. A consenting client is followed by platform verification. Formal Team preauthorizes the known secondary worker.
  4. Hidden Teams adds another option. Workers compare hidden collaboration’s expected benefit with coordination cost and expected detection penalties. Oversight strengthens liability enforcement and detection, and costs the platform. These opposing incentives can have different effects on reassignment and backup.
  5. Profit is an account, not a bonus score. Successful transactions generate contribution. Failed transactions, extra secondary-failure exposure, approval processing, oversight and undetected hidden use create costs. An opportunity that never forms is not a failed transaction. Worker penalties are not counted as platform revenue.
  6. Compare the same opportunities. Capability, value, private costs and event-specific random draws are fixed by a marketplace code. Decisions use expected outcomes, never future success draws. A different policy changes participation, not the underlying lottery. The comparison is for this sample, not a universal policy ranking.

Jobs are independent, sequential opportunities with a known two-person worker pair. There is no queue, capacity competition, strategic client pricing or upfront team-registration choice. A formed transaction ends in exactly one success or failure, even when it has two attempts. Hidden decisions are revealed to you as an educational observer after commitment; this does not give the platform advance knowledge.

Use the policy cards with Tab and arrow keys. Pause and resume at any time; Next job finishes the current animation, and Skip to results finishes the round. Leaving this tab pauses playback. Reduced-motion preferences remove moving transitions without hiding the explanations.
