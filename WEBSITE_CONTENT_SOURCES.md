## Dispatch Dash: Beat the Clock! — approved release, 18 October 2026

The user supplied a separate brief requesting a real-time single-courier routing game with 1-, 3-, and 5-minute sessions. On 18 October 2026 the user explicitly approved this version, including the Interactive Lab card, adjusted arrivals, extra deadline time and visible courier body, for commit, push and live verification. The archived three-round staffing simulator remains untouched and unpublished.

All restaurant/customer names, map locations, orders, arrivals, deadlines, road travel times, scores, and teaching scenarios are fictional. No real platform data, third-party maps, personal location data, or unpublished research were used. Original editable SVG city and otter bicycle-courier artwork was created for this game. The otter follows the explicit new game brief; the homepage's existing cat artwork is unchanged.

The illustrative model uses a 5-by-4 road grid, 31 bidirectional road segments, Dijkstra routing, a capacity of three orders, and seeded arrivals and bursts. At the user's request, three seconds are added after computing each original 12–35-second allowance, giving 15–38-second deadlines and a 38-second final clearing period. Road lengths are 200 or 170 map units, traversed in 2 or 1.7 game seconds. These are game-scale assumptions, not empirical travel speeds. Each delivery earns +1 on or before its deadline; each undelivered order loses one point when it expires. Backup couriers are an explicit queue-removal abstraction.

At the user's subsequent request to reduce incoming orders, the interval between waves was initially doubled, then adjusted to 1.65 times the original gap. After another playtest, the user requested a slightly slower pace. The multiplier is now 1.8: 9–11.16 seconds between waves for one-minute sessions and 12.6–16.2 seconds for longer sessions, about 8% fewer waves per second than the previous version. Opening orders still appear immediately and waves can still contain simultaneous orders. These intervals remain illustrative game assumptions.

Game results explain routing, deadline prioritization, and batching trade-offs without making empirical claims. On-time rate uses all generated orders as its denominator. Distance per delivery includes all travel and is undefined if no delivery completes. Optional hints use road travel estimates and known active orders; future requests are not disclosed. The game engine uses no storage, network API calls or research datasets. A subsequent user-requested Cloudflare Web Analytics installation is described below.

## Crowd Verdict: Refund or Reject? — approved release, 20 October 2026

The user supplied the complete brief and approved the finished local version for publication and live verification on 20 October 2026. The third Interactive Lab card opens a standalone fictional crowd-judging simulation. It explicitly does not reproduce the theoretical model in *Rushed Justice* or assess real legal refund entitlement.

Players start with ten fictional tokens and judge up to ten disputes. A one-token entry fee reveals original product evidence; majority agreement awards two tokens. Every simulated juror's type and ballot is generated before the player's vote. The player is included in an odd total of three, five, seven or nine voters. Replaying a seed preserves the evidence and other jurors' votes, without a forced winning rate. Normal-voter probabilities, buyer/seller leanings, variety sampling and the illustrative damage benchmark are disclosed game assumptions, not empirical research findings.

All ten product categories, four condition variants per product, animal jurors and jury-room artwork are original editable SVG. Product descriptions and complaints are fictional. ## Solo or Team restoration and gameplay balance — 21 October 2026

The owner subsequently requested restoring the earlier platform-governance game after Crowd Verdict and approved this revision for commit, push and live verification. The nine runtime files retain the original characters, glowing route animation and three challenges with ten opportunities each. The separate five-job redesign and randomized-model experiment remain archived and excluded.

At the owner's request, the detailed profit-breakdown panel is removed from results. All three policies are tuned for approximately equal realized full-game winning frequencies at the default controls. Approval processing is 6.6 + 5s² credits per submitted proposal. A seeded 30–110 credit amount shared across the marketplace is added to each opportunity's original 6–18 credit loss for a failed secondary attempt. Fresh visits generate a new code; comparisons and replay share the same underlying opportunities and outcome draws. There is no winner selection, outcome resampling or policy bonus.

Independent validation over 10,000 separate marketplace codes found fractional win shares of 33.94% Block, 32.84% Case-by-Case Approval and 33.22% Formal Team. Ties occurred in 5.68% of marketplaces and were split evenly for this statistic. Controls were fixed at 40% approval requirements and 35% oversight, not optimized. These are intentionally tuned gameplay results, not empirical estimates or findings from the paper. Equal winning frequency does not imply equal expected or average profit, and individual challenges or other settings can have different rankings. Academic content and the other two games are unchanged.
